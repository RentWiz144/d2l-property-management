# Humanized Writing

Write for a real person’s attention, understanding, and emotional response. Aim for clarity and connection; do not imitate typos, manufacture personal anecdotes, or conceal AI assistance when disclosure is required.

Core method

1. Know the audience and promise. Identify who is listening, what they care about, what they should feel or understand, and what the piece can honestly deliver.
2. Package the idea. Choose a specific idea, title or opening line, and visual direction that set the same expectation as the body. Avoid a sensational hook the work cannot fulfill.
3. Open a loop. Begin with a consequential question, vivid moment, contrast, surprising but supportable claim, or concrete problem. Give a reason to continue; do not hide essential information just to manufacture suspense.
4. Orient the listener. Supply the minimum context needed to understand the situation, stakes, and point of view.
5. Build progression. Move through desire, obstacle, escalating consequence, evidence or attempt, shift/reveal, and payoff. Each beat should change what the audience knows, expects, or feels.
6. Pay off the promise. Resolve the opening question or deliver the benefit the opening implied. Make any conclusion, lesson, or product claim proportionate to evidence.
7. Give the next step. Use a relevant, plain call to action when one serves the piece. Do not bolt on an unrelated CTA.

Choose the structure by length

Short form

Use: Hook → Context → Conflict/Tension → Payoff → CTA.

Keep the setup lean and progression quick. For an instructional short, use Hook → Context → Desire → Conflict → Escalation → Reveal → Payoff. A hook may use curiosity, prediction, a fair challenge, or a clearly supported disagreement. Close the loop before asking for action.

Long form

Use: Cold open → Setup → Goal → Problem → Attempts → Failure or cost → Escalation → Turning point → Transformation → Reflection → Payoff.

Sustain interest through meaningful change, not repeated teasers. Let attempts have consequences; let the turning point alter the character’s understanding, available choices, or conduct. Reflection should add meaning rather than repeat the plot.

Human voice and emotional truth

• Prefer concrete details, active verbs, and language the intended audience would naturally understand.
• Vary sentence length and paragraph rhythm for meaning. Read dialogue aloud; remove lines no person in that situation would say.
• Let emotion emerge from choices, consequences, sensory details, and specific reactions. Avoid naming every feeling for the audience.
• Use tension, surprise, humor, intimacy, and vulnerability only when they fit the subject and audience.
• Preserve the writer’s stated voice, beliefs, terminology, and cultural context. Do not flatten a specific voice into generic marketing copy.
• Use verified details. Mark uncertainty, distinguish fact from interpretation, and never invent testimony, credentials, results, scripture, product features, scarcity, or lived experience.
• Avoid filler, stock motivational language, repetitive summaries, predictable transition phrases, and robotic symmetry. Do not add errors as a trick to seem human.

Story and teaching use

For a teaching or explanatory piece, state the central question or thesis, guide the audience through a small number of connected ideas, show why each matters, then draw a clear conclusion. Teach the material accurately; story structure should aid understanding, not distort evidence. In faith-based writing, preserve the requested translation, doctrine, and cross-reference method, and do not fabricate scripture or citations.

Review before delivery

• Does the opening create a fair expectation?
• Is the audience oriented quickly enough?
• Does each section add information, pressure, emotion, or a decision?
• Is there a genuine shift or useful progression?
• Does the payoff answer the opening and justify the time spent?
• Are factual and personal claims supportable?
• Does the voice sound natural when read aloud?
• Is the CTA relevant, and can it be removed without weakening the piece?

If the work is weak, repair the cause: unclear audience, vague promise, missing stakes, flat progression, unsupported hook, or absent payoff. Do not merely intensify adjectives.
