# Platform-Native Specs

One idea. Five different pieces of writing. This file is the difference
between cross-posting and actually showing up on each network.

**The rule:** if you can paste the caption from one platform into another and
it reads fine, you haven't done the work.

---

## The shared core

Before splitting, lock these down once:

- **The idea** — one sentence, plain. "Your testimony is listed as a weapon."
- **The Scripture** — one verse, KJV, with the precept spelled out.
- **The product tie** — at most one item, and only if it belongs. A forced
  product line reads as a forced product line.
- **The ask** — what one thing should a person do?

Then write each platform from that core. Not from each other.

---

## Instagram

**Format:** Feed post, carousel, Reel, Story. Reels get the most cold reach;
carousels get the most saves.

**The first 125 characters are the whole game.** That's what shows before "more."
Land the hook there or lose the scroll.

**Length:** 125-char hook, then 3–6 short paragraphs. Line breaks are oxygen —
a wall of text gets skipped regardless of quality.

**Hashtags:** 8–15, in the **first comment**, not the caption. Mix three tiers:
- Broad (1M+): `#biblestudy` `#faith` `#modestfashion`
- Mid (100K–1M): `#israelites` `#kjv` `#plussizefashion`
- Narrow / owned (<100K): `#chozen` `#thechozencollection` `#preceptuponprecept`

Narrow tags are where a small account actually ranks. Don't skip them for
volume.

**CTA:** One. Comment-bait outperforms link-bait on IG because the platform
suppresses outbound links. "Drop a one-line testimony below" beats "link in bio."

**Alt text:** Always. It's accessibility and it's indexed. Describe the garment
including the fringes and ribband — that's both correct and searchable.

**ChoZen note:** Modesty rules make ChoZen visually distinct in a feed full of
fitted athleisure. Frame it as the point, not a constraint.

## TikTok

**Format:** Video, near-always. Photo carousels work but video is the engine.

**Length:** Caption is **short**. 100–150 characters. The hook lives in the
first two seconds of *video*, not in text. A long TikTok caption is a tell that
someone pasted from Facebook.

**The first 2 seconds decide everything.** Open on motion, a face, or a claim.
Never a logo. Never a slow fade.

**Hashtags:** 3–5, in the caption. TikTok's algorithm reads content far better
than tags, so tags are context, not distribution. `#chozen` plus 2–4 relevant.

**Sound:** Trending audio meaningfully lifts reach. Check it fresh — sounds
turn over in days. Original voiceover works when the hook is strong, and for
ChoZen it's often better because the message is the product.

**CTA:** Spoken in the video, not just written. "Comment your size" out loud.

**Captions/subtitles:** Burn them in. Most viewing is sound-off.

**ChoZen note:** Verify "ChoZen" is pronounced like "chosen" in every
voiceover before upload. This is the single most common failure and it is
unrecoverable once posted.

## Facebook

**Format:** Image post, Reel, Story. The audience skews older and warmer —
more existing customers, more family, more church network.

**Length:** Facebook tolerates long. It's the one place a full testimony
lands. 200–500 words is fine when it's a real story.

**Hashtags:** 0–2, or none. Facebook barely uses them. Twelve hashtags on a
Facebook post look like automation, because they are.

**The hook** should be conversational, not punchy. Facebook is a room, not a
stage. "I want to tell you what happened this week" outperforms "STOP
SCROLLING."

**Links** work here better than any other network. This is where a product
link earns its place.

**CTA:** Direct questions get comments. Comments get distribution.

**ChoZen note:** This is the warm-audience channel. Testimony content — the
strokes, the stents, why the company exists — belongs here in full. It's too
long for TikTok and too heavy for Threads.

---

## Threads

**Format:** Text-first, images optional. The most conversational network of
the five.

**Length:** Under 500 characters. Shorter is better. One thought.

**Hashtags:** One, maximum. Threads treats them as topic tags, not reach.

**Voice:** Threads punishes polish. It rewards the thing you'd actually say.
"Nothing fit me at 6'5" and 275, so we made it" is a Threads post. A press
release is not.

**Threading:** Break a longer thought into a genuine reply chain. Each part
should stand alone.

**CTA:** Ask something real. Threads users reply more than any other network.

**ChoZen note:** This account is connected and has been receiving nothing.
Cheapest channel to activate — a Threads post is often just the honest core of
an Instagram caption with the marketing sanded off.

---

## YouTube

**Format:** Long-form video or Shorts. Different products, different strategy.

**Title:** The whole ballgame. 60 characters. Front-load the searchable term.
"How to Study the Bible: Precept Upon Precept (Isaiah 28:10)" beats "The Bible
Tells You HOW to Study It."

**Description:** First 150 characters show in search. Then the full
description — 200+ words, keyword-rich, timestamps for anything over 4 minutes,
links at the bottom.

**Tags:** 10–15, in the API `tags` field.

**Thumbnail:** Matters more than the title. A face with an expression beats
text-on-image. Must be readable at phone size.

**Shorts:** Vertical, under 60s, hook in 1 second. Effectively TikTok with a
different audience — but re-cut, don't re-upload with a watermark.

**ChoZen note:** YouTube is the monetization channel and it's connected and
idle. It's also the only network here where content compounds — a Bible study
video keeps earning views for years. TikTok content is dead in 72 hours.

## Quick reference

| | Instagram | TikTok | Facebook | Threads | YouTube |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Caption length | 125-char hook + short paras | 100–150 chars | 200–500 words | <500 chars | 200+ word description |
| Hashtags | 8–15, first comment | 3–5, in caption | 0–2 | 0–1 | 10–15 in tags field |
| Hook lives in | First 125 chars | First 2s of video | Conversational open | The first line | Title + thumbnail |
| Links | Suppressed | Suppressed | **Work well** | Weak | Description |
| Best CTA | Comment prompt | Spoken in video | Direct question | Real question | Subscribe + comment |
| Content life | ~48 hours | ~72 hours | ~24 hours | ~12 hours | **Years** |

## Adaptation worked example

**Core idea:** Revelation 12:11 — your testimony is listed as a weapon.

**Instagram** (hook in 125, hashtags to first comment):
> Your testimony is listed as a weapon. The verse says so.
>
> Revelation 12:11 names two instruments of overcoming — the blood of the
> Lamb, and the word of their testimony. Your story isn't small talk. It's
> listed weaponry.
>
> The precept: a testimony kept silent is a weapon left in its sheath.
>
> Drop a one-line testimony below. What did God bring you through? We read
> every one.

**TikTok** (short; the work is in the video):
> A testimony kept silent is a weapon left in the sheath. Revelation 12:11.
> What did God bring you through? 👇
>
> #chozen #testimony #kjv #biblestudy

**Facebook** (long, warm, personal):
> I want to tell you why we put Revelation 12:11 on a shirt.
>
> Three strokes. Three heart stents. A brain stent in my basilar artery. Lung
> surgery for a mass that — thank God — wasn't cancer. I'm still here, and the
> verse says the reason people overcome is two things: the blood of the Lamb,
> and the word of their testimony.
>
> Not the blood alone. The telling matters too.
>
> [continues — full testimony, ends with a direct question]

**Threads** (one thought, no polish):
> Revelation 12:11 lists your testimony as a weapon. Not a nice story. A
> weapon.
>
> So what did God bring you through? I'll go first.

**YouTube Short** (title carries it):
> Title: Your Testimony Is a Weapon — Revelation 12:11 Explained
> Description: Revelation 12:11 names two instruments of overcoming...

Five posts. One idea. Nothing copy-pasted.
