Read this carefully

More sessions reached checkout (46) than added to a cart (13). That gap means most buyers are using express buttons — Shop Pay, PayPal — that skip the cart entirely. The clearest example

The Set Apart Embossed Tee is your flagship logo piece. What sells can't be bought. What can be bought isn't sold.

The plan · dependency-ordered

Seven phases, in this order

Each phase multiplies the ones after it. Doing phase 6 before phase 1 means pouring traffic into a store that can't accept it — which is what's happening today.

Phase 0This week · ~4 hrs

Triage the obvious breakage

Nothing strategic here. These are things that are simply wrong right now.

  • Four active products have no image at all — Final Exodus Tee ($55), Women's Embossed Maxi Dress ($99.99), Stronger Temple 40 ($59), Fringing Service ($7). They render as blank tiles in collections. The Final Exodus Tee is worst: it's genuinely purchasable (CONTINUE policy) and selling with no photograph. Shoot them or set them to draft — a live product without an image should not exist.
  • Fix the Deuteronomy price contradiction. Collection pages advertise "from $99.99"; the White-on-White variants ring up at $110.00. On your single best-selling product, that discrepancy lands at the exact moment of decision.
  • Draft the Women's Embossed Maxi Dress. One variant, size 3XL only, no image, no stock. It is a broken listing sitting at $99.99.
  • Pull seven empty collections out of navigation — ChoZen Luxury, Limited Edition, and all four Studio 144 sub-collections plus Studio 144 itself. All contain zero products. Every click into them is a dead end.
  • Delete the duplicate. "Home & Visuals" exists twice with identical rules (home-visuals and home-visuals-1), splitting its own SEO.
  • Delete the $0.00 placeholder product literally titled "Digital product", and the archived -OLD track suit SKUs.
Phase 1Week 1–2

Collapse the variant matrix

This is the whole game. One rule: a colourway stays live only if S through 2XL can actually be bought. Anything else gets archived or converted to a real pre-order.

  • Archive dead colourways. Skyline goes from 104 variants to roughly 15. Deuteronomy from 95 to about 18 — protect this one, it's 29% of revenue. The men's Windbreaker has 4 units across 84 variants; cut to one colourway.
  • Convert proven-but-empty products to pre-order rather than deleting them. Switch inventory policy from DENY to CONTINUE, add a pre-order badge and a dated ship window. This applies to the Modest Set, Flex Cargo Maxi, King's Short Set, and both Set Apart tees — all have demand and no stock.
  • Fix the promise-mechanics mismatch. The Deuteronomy description already reads "PREORDER | EXPECTED PRODUCTION TIME: 3–4 WEEKS", but its variants are set to DENY. You are promising pre-orders while the cart refuses them. The Final Exodus Tee is configured correctly — copy that pattern.
  • Lead with the healthy SKUs. Windbreaker for Her (65 units), Heavyweight Tee (38), Ephod Ring (28) are the only apparel you can sell at volume today. They should be the first things a visitor sees.
Phase 2Week 2

Give the catalog a spine

Most products carry no tags at all, and several collections are smart collections keyed on tags. The catalog is invisible to its own automation.

  • Tag every product on four axes: audience (mens/womens/unisex), category (tee, dress, set, canvas, digital…), availability (in-stock/preorder), and line (chozen-collection, chozen-visuals, chozen-digital).
  • Men's Apparel and Women's Apparel repair themselves the moment you do. They currently hold 3 and 7 products out of ~30, purely because the tags they filter on don't exist.
  • Rewrite the main collection rule. "THE ChoZen COLLECTION" currently filters by excluding title strings — NOT_CONTAINS "Premium", "Bundle", "Narroway", "Exclusive" — plus price ≠ 199. That rule silently drops both $199 Visuals canvases, the Premium Track Suit and the Narroway Canvas from your primary collection. Rebuild it on tags.
  • Stronger Temple 40 is in no digital collection. Its type is "Wellness Program" and vendor is "Covenant Wellness", so it matches neither the ChoZen Digital rule (vendor-based) nor Digital & Music (type-based). A $59 product at ~100% margin is orphaned.
Phase 3Week 2–3

Sell the thirteen things that never run out

Ten canvases and three digital products have unlimited availability and have earned nothing in twelve months. While apparel restocks, this is your entire sellable inventory.

  • Put a canvas and a digital product above the fold on the homepage. Right now the store leads with apparel a visitor mostly cannot buy.
  • Treat the $7 handbook as the front door, not a revenue line. At $7 its job is to convert a stranger into a buyer and an email address; apparel is the follow-up. That is also the cheapest possible fix for your dead email list.
  • Give Stronger Temple 40 a start date and a cap. It's described as a cohort, but it's listed as an always-open product with no image and no urgency. A cohort without a date is just a PDF.
  • The canvases are your most searchable asset — "Lion of Judah canvas", "Psalm 107:2 wall art". Nothing else in the catalog has that kind of intent behind it. See phase 6.
Phase 4Week 3

Raise order value with what you already built

  • The Fringing Service is the best product nobody can find. $7–$10, grounded in Numbers 15:38, untracked, always available, correctly configured — and it has no image and no placement. It should appear as a checkbox on every garment page and again in the cart. At 32 orders it would have added roughly $270 at near-100% margin this year, for zero additional inventory.
  • Rebuild the bundles. The Set Apart Tee Bundle is live with zero stock; the for-Her bundle is stuck in draft. Both are dead weight. Build bundles only from stockable pieces.
  • Target AOV $97 → $130. Fringing attach plus one real bundle gets you most of the way.
Phase 5Week 3–4

Feed the email engine you already own

Klaviyo has six live flows — Abandoned Cart, Browse Abandonment, Welcome, SMS Welcome, Winback, Order Confirmed — and eight lists. In 90 days email drove two sessions. The machine isn't broken. Nothing is entering it.

  • You have four lead-magnet lists already created — The Chosen 144, Hype Check → Covenant Wellness, 500 Prompt Book, DEVCAS Waitlist — and no on-site path into any of them. Wire up capture with a real offer.
  • 87% checkout abandonment means the abandoned-cart flow has fuel the moment capture improves. Those 40 abandoned checkouts are the highest-intent audience you have.
  • Send one campaign a week. Email is 0.1% of your traffic. For a brand this identity-driven it should be 20–30%.
Phase 6Ongoing

Build traffic that compounds

Search brought 87 sessions in 90 days — 4.5%. Direct (64%) plus social (31%) is traffic that stops the day you stop posting. Nothing accrues.

  • Your product descriptions target nothing. "Step into purpose with…" appears near-verbatim across multiple listings. Rewrite against real queries: "modest athletic maxi dress", "1 Peter 2:9 shirt", "Lion of Judah canvas", "Deuteronomy 22:5 dress".
  • "Tzitzit fringing service" is a genuine opening. High intent, almost no e-commerce competition, and you already offer it. Nobody is ranking for it.
Phase 7After phase 1

Where the publishing system plugs in

The publishing engine is the last piece, not the first. Aimed at today's store it would drive visitors into a catalog where four in five options are empty — spending reach to produce bounces.

  • Point campaigns only at what's buyable: Windbreaker for Her, Heavyweight Tee, Ephod Ring, the ten canvases, the three digital products.
  • Hold campaigns off the Set Apart Tee, Modest Set, King's Short Set and Flex Cargo Maxi until they're restocked or genuinely on pre-order. Promoting them today sends people to a wall.
  • Every post should land on a page that can take money. That is the only rule that matters here.
